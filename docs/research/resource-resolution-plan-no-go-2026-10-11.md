## Actual lookup contract: why high repetition is not a cache license

For the current 1.21.1 source owner, `MultiPackResourceManager` routes by namespace to `FallbackResourceManager`. Its point lookup walks entries from high to low priority; real `PackResources.getResource(type, id)` calls may be observable supplier discovery, and filter-only entries execute predicates. The stack route has its own supplier/metadata-filter/ordering behavior. The successful `Resource` contains a *lazy* metadata finder, so callbacks may occur after the public method returns. The owning list of pack entries is not a proof of frozen providers or filesystem contents. A manager generation identity cannot invalidate mutable providers, predicates, live ZIP/directory state, or later supplier opens.

Consequently a memoized hit/miss, negative miss set, or per-namespace list of `known` providers would eliminate stock callbacks. A frozen `pack`-order snapshot could also differ from live entries/filters; it strays into agent 215's catalog owner. Even when a test corpus has 38k repeated keys, semantic equivalence requires the **original calls, in the original order, with the same receivers, arguments, exception points, values, resource/lazy metadata/supplier freshness, and lifetime**. Null `Resource.source` is legitimate for some generated shaders; see #71.
